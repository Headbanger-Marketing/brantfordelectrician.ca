A panel and an electrical service are different parts of the system

The panel distributes electricity to property circuits. The service is the incoming supply and associated equipment. Replacing a panel does not automatically increase the available supply. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess existing condition and demand, planned loads and the utility requirements before recommending equipment. Spare breaker spaces alone do not establish capacity.

Prepare the scope for your Brantford property

Gather the inspection report or insurer letter and copy out the exact wording of the concern. Take a photograph of the closed panel door and its labels from outside, and list the heat pump, range or charger you plan to add.

Ask whether the concern is about the panel itself, the incoming supply or the way circuits were added later. Ask what the insurer or lender needs to see in writing once the work is finished.

What should the assessment establish?

After a home inspection or an insurer letter has flagged a Brantford panel, you choose whether to agree to the proposed work, on the strength of a scope that records what was actually observed and what the concern refers to. In it, a condition issue with existing equipment is kept separate from the question of whether the supply suits the loads you plan to add together. Demand reasoning appears in plain terms, naming each planned item it covers. Whether the utility or an ESA notification is involved is stated. The document the insurer can be given is named as well.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Does an insurer letter about a panel mean it must be replaced?

Not necessarily. The letter may describe a condition, an age or a missing record, and each has a different remedy. Ask the contractor to assess the panel and explain which concern, if any, needs work.

Who can answer a home inspector's note about my Brantford panel?

A Licensed Electrical Contractor can assess the panel and respond in writing. The inspector who wrote the note can clarify what was seen, but only the contractor can say what work, if any, should follow.

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
