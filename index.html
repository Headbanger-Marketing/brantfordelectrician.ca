Before work begins

Add up the planned loads before choosing any one.

A home with a driveway or parking pad but no garage may need an outdoor wall, a post or a long cable route from the indoor panel for equipment that a garage would normally hold. Other homes can have the same need.

List every upgrade you expect, such as a heat pump, an induction range and a vehicle charger, and note the exterior spots each could use. Ask the contractor to assess the combined demand once, rather than one project at a time.

Should a heat pump, range and charger be assessed together?

Yes, a single assessment of the combined demand is more useful than three separate ones. Ask the contractor to list each planned item, state what the existing supply and panel were assessed against and explain the order of work.

What records help when I compare quotes for several upgrades?

Gather product sheets for each planned appliance, any inspection report and earlier ESA paperwork. Give every contractor the same list, so the quotes describe the same scope and can be compared line by line.

Can upgrades be done in stages without redoing earlier work?

Staging can suit a budget, provided the early work does not make later work harder. Ask the contractor to describe the whole plan, then identify which parts of each stage prepare for equipment you have not bought yet.

Does a charger on a parking pad need different equipment?

It may. A charger outside a garage is exposed to rain and temperature changes, so ask whether outdoor-rated equipment is needed and where the supply cable would run from the indoor panel to the mounting spot.

Who signs for the work after I send my project details?

Keep signing between you and a provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor. Read this site as planning guides: it receives and reviews your enquiry but sets up no visit or price and does no work. Check who the business is and its ECRA/ESA licence before agreeing, then settle scope and price with it directly, along with the timing.
